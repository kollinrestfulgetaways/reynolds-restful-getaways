Travel rewards can save a lot of money, but the system is very good at making people feel like they are winning even when they are spending more than they planned.

These are the beginner mistakes I would work hardest to avoid.

## Opening a card without a redemption plan

A large welcome offer is not a travel strategy.

Before applying, I want at least a rough idea of which trip or type of redemption the points could support.

That does not mean every point has to be assigned immediately. It means I understand the currency I am earning.

## Carrying a balance to earn rewards

Interest can erase the value of points quickly.

If I cannot pay the statement balance in full, the rewards are not the priority.

## Spending extra to hit a welcome offer

A bonus is not free if you buy things you did not need to qualify for it.

I prefer timing applications around normal large expenses and routine household spending.

## Transferring points before finding availability

Flexible points are valuable partly because they are flexible.

Once transferred to an airline or hotel program, they generally cannot simply be moved back.

I search first, confirm the award, check transfer timing, and only then move points.

## Assuming every referral link is the best offer

Referral links can support creators, including this site, but the referral offer can be worse than an issuer's public offer.

I would always compare both before applying.

## Ignoring annual fees after the first year

The first-year value can be very different from the long-term value.

Every renewal is a chance to ask whether the card still earns its spot.

## Valuing every benefit at face value

A $200 credit is not automatically worth $200 to me.

If I would not have spent that money without the card, I discount the value heavily or count it as zero.

## Hoarding points forever

Points are not an investment account.

Programs can change award pricing, partner relationships, and rules. I like earning with a purpose and redeeming when a good trip appears.

## Obsessing over a perfect cents-per-point value

A mathematically impressive redemption is not useful if it is for a trip you do not want.

I compare points with the cash price I would actually pay and think about what the points could do on my next realistic trip.

## Making the system too complicated

The hobby can become a spreadsheet full of credits, transfer partners, certificates, and deadlines.

More cards do not automatically mean more value.

I would rather manage a smaller system well than build a huge one that causes missed payments, wasted benefits, or unnecessary annual fees.

## The simple version

My rules are pretty straightforward:

- Pay in full.
- Spend normally.
- Know why you want the points.
- Search before transferring.
- Track annual fees.
- Compare referral and public offers.
- Use points for trips you actually want.

If the rewards strategy makes your finances or travel more stressful, simplify it.

The goal is not to win the points game. The goal is to use rewards to make real travel more affordable and enjoyable.
