Tokyo was one of those trips that reminded me why I spend so much time learning points and miles.

After points helped us make our first big dream trip to Paris happen, Japan felt like the next chapter. This time, we were able to use a mix of **Chase points, Hyatt, and American Airlines miles** to take a trip that would have been much more expensive if we had paid cash for every major piece.

For our summer trip, we transferred **Chase points to Hyatt** and booked **Hyatt House Tokyo Shibuya**. For the flights, we used **American Airlines miles** to fly **Japan Airlines premium economy on the way to Japan** and **American Airlines business class on the way home**.

Across the parts of the trip we booked with rewards, we used roughly **200,000 points and miles total**.

The number is fun to talk about, but the reason I care about it is what those points unlocked: a base in Shibuya, a more comfortable way to cross the Pacific, and days in Tokyo that felt completely different from anywhere we had traveled before.

## Our Tokyo points snapshot

Here is the simple version of how we put the trip together:

- **Hotel:** Hyatt House Tokyo Shibuya
- **Hotel currency:** Chase points transferred to World of Hyatt
- **Outbound flight:** Japan Airlines premium economy
- **Return flight:** American Airlines business class
- **Flight currency:** American Airlines miles
- **Total rewards used:** about 200,000 points and miles

I like this trip as an example because it was not about finding one perfect redemption. We combined different programs and different cabins to build the trip we actually wanted.

## Why Shibuya made sense for us

We wanted Tokyo to feel like Tokyo from the moment we walked outside, and staying in Shibuya gave us that kind of base.

The Hachiko statue was one of those places we had seen countless times in photos and videos, so getting our own picture there made the trip feel real immediately. Shibuya also gave us a starting point for exploring the rest of the city instead of treating the hotel as the destination.

For the hotel, the important points lesson was simple: **Chase points gave us options.** Because Chase Ultimate Rewards can transfer to Hyatt, the points we had earned on a flexible currency could become hotel nights when we were ready to book.

That is the kind of flexibility I want from a points strategy. I would rather earn points I can use for a trip I genuinely want than chase a big balance without a plan for it.

## We mixed cabins instead of forcing one perfect award

Our flights are probably my favorite part of the strategy behind this trip.

We flew **Japan Airlines premium economy to Japan** and **American Airlines business class on the way home**, using American Airlines miles for the award bookings.

It would have been easy to decide that the trip only counted as a win if we found business class both ways. I do not think about points that way.

Premium economy gave us extra comfort for the long flight to Japan while letting us make the itinerary work with the award space we could actually book. Then we used business class for the trip home.

That flexibility is something I think more people should be comfortable with. The best award trip is not always the one with the fanciest cabin on every segment. Sometimes it is the trip that uses the points efficiently enough that you actually get to go.

## Tokyo felt like several trips in one

One of the things that stood out to us was how quickly Tokyo could change from one neighborhood or experience to another.

We had the huge signs and crowds around Shibuya, the Hachiko statue, colorful storefronts, and city views that felt almost futuristic. Then we could be surrounded by temple architecture, giant lanterns, traditional clothing, or rows of red torii gates.

Some of the moments we captured from the trip included visiting the **Senso-ji and Kaminarimon area**, seeing the pagoda, spending time around Shibuya, and getting to experience more traditional parts of Japanese culture alongside the modern city.

That contrast is a big part of what made Tokyo memorable for us.

## The hotel transfer is why I value flexible Chase points

This trip is also a good example of why I do not look at a Chase point as having one fixed use.

When we were ready to book the hotel, we could transfer the points to Hyatt and use them toward Hyatt House Tokyo Shibuya. The points stopped being a number in an account and became part of a real trip.

That is the point where this hobby makes sense to me.

I am not trying to collect the largest number of points possible. I want enough flexibility to look at a trip and ask, **what combination of points, miles, and cash makes the most sense here?**

For Japan, Hyatt and American Airlines miles were the pieces that fit.

## What I would repeat from this booking strategy

If I were planning another long-haul points trip, I would absolutely use the same general approach:

- Start with the destination instead of starting with a credit card.
- Check multiple cabin options instead of demanding business class every direction.
- Use transferable points for hotels or flights only after finding a redemption worth booking.
- Mix programs when it creates a better overall itinerary.
- Keep enough flexibility that the points strategy serves the trip, not the other way around.

That last part has become the theme of Reynolds Restful Getaways for me: **travel first, rewards second.**

## From Paris to Tokyo

Paris showed me what points and miles could make possible. Tokyo proved it was not a one-time thing.

The more I learned, the more our travel options changed. A trip across the world stopped feeling like something we could only dream about or put off indefinitely. It became a planning problem I could actually work through: where should we stay, which program has the flight, which points transfer where, and how can we build the best trip from the options available?

That does not make the trip free. We still spend real money on food, local transportation, activities, taxes, fees, and everything else that comes with traveling.

But taking major costs like flights and hotels and attacking them with points can completely change what feels possible.

## The takeaway

We used roughly **200,000 points and miles** across the reward bookings for our Tokyo trip. Those rewards helped us stay at Hyatt House Tokyo Shibuya, fly Japan Airlines premium economy to Japan, and fly American Airlines business class home.

But just like Paris, the best value was not a cents-per-point calculation.

It was getting to go.

It was standing in Shibuya together, seeing Tokyo in the rain, visiting temples and shrines, and coming home with another trip that we probably would have approached very differently before I learned how points and miles work.

That is the value I want to keep chasing.
