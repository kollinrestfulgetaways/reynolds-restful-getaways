Trying to fit Scotland and England into one week can turn into a checklist trip very quickly. My priority would be keeping the route simple: **start in Edinburgh, make one worthwhile stop on the way south, and finish in London.**

That gives you three distinct parts of the trip without packing and unpacking every night.

## The route

For a seven-night trip, I would divide it roughly like this:

- Edinburgh: 3 nights
- York: 1 night
- London: 3 nights

You could give York an extra night and take one away from Edinburgh or London, but I like this split for a first visit.

## Days 1-3: Edinburgh

I would stay somewhere reasonably central so the first few days can be mostly on foot.

For the first day, I would keep the schedule light. Walk the Royal Mile, explore Old Town, get a good dinner, and avoid booking anything that becomes stressful if the flight is late.

The next two days could include:

- Edinburgh Castle
- Victoria Street and Grassmarket
- Dean Village
- Calton Hill
- Arthur's Seat if the weather cooperates
- A whisky experience or pub evening
- Stockbridge and the Water of Leith

Three nights gives you enough time to see the major sights without feeling like you have to sprint through them.

## Day 4: Train to York

York works well because it breaks up the trip and is directly on the rail route toward London.

I would take a morning train, leave bags at the hotel, and spend the rest of the day walking the city.

The priorities for a short stop would be:

- York Minster
- The Shambles
- Walking part of the city walls
- A pub dinner
- An evening walk after the day-trip crowds thin out

One night is short, but York's compact center makes it practical.

## Days 5-7: London

I would take an early train from York to London and stay in London for the remainder of the trip.

Instead of trying to see every famous landmark, I would group each day geographically.

One day could focus on Westminster, St. James's Park, Buckingham Palace, and the South Bank.

Another could center on the Tower of London, Tower Bridge, Borough Market, and the City.

The third could be more flexible: museums, Notting Hill, Greenwich, a West End show, or a neighborhood you specifically want to explore.

## Why I would use trains instead of flying

For this route, rail keeps the trip simpler.

You avoid traveling out to an airport, arriving early for security, and then traveling back into another city after landing. The train also lets the route itself become part of the trip.

I would still compare fares and schedules before booking, especially because advance rail pricing can vary significantly.

## Where points can help

For a trip like this, I would first look at using points for the expensive long-haul flights and potentially the London hotel nights.

I would not force points into every part of the trip. A reasonably priced independent hotel in York may be a better use of cash than transferring points just because I have them.

That is one of my favorite rules for rewards travel: **use points where they solve an expensive problem.** Pay cash where cash is already reasonable.

## How I would book it

My booking order would be:

- International flights
- London hotel
- Edinburgh hotel
- York hotel
- Intercity trains
- Any timed attractions that are must-dos
- Restaurants or shows that require advance reservations

I would leave some meals and sightseeing open so the trip still feels like a vacation.

## A note on flying open-jaw

If the price works, I would strongly consider flying **into Edinburgh and home from London** instead of returning to Edinburgh just to catch the flight home.

That can save a full travel day and makes the itinerary flow naturally south.

When searching with points, I would price the two directions separately as well as together. One loyalty program may be best for the outbound flight while another is better for the return.

## The version I would avoid

I would not try to add Glasgow, the Highlands, York, Liverpool, Bath, and London into the same seven-day trip.

Those are all worthwhile places, but the cost is time. Every extra destination adds another checkout, another transit day, and another chance for the trip to feel rushed.

For one week, I would rather come home feeling like I experienced three places than feeling like I collected seven train stations.
